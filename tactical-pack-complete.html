5-3-2 // 3-5-2 — DIV 2 VICTORIAN CHURCHES
The Idea

Why This Works

We play a 5-3-2 when we don't have the ball and a 3-5-2 when we do. The wing-backs do the switching — high when we have it, dropped in when we don't. Same eleven players, two shapes.

The plan is simple: compact in the middle, force them wide, press together when the trigger comes, run the channels when we win it back. The high line shrinks the pitch so they can't play through us. The wide CBs have pace because that's what makes the high line possible.

Most goals come from one of three things: pressing them into mistakes, hitting them on transition, or wing-back crosses. Everything else is keeping the ball.

Four Team-Wide Rules

  • One trigger, one job. Don't chase everything.
  • Press together or don't press at all. High line + no press = goals against.
  • The high line steps and drops as ONE unit. Central CB calls it.
  • Talk constantly — especially when we don't have the ball. We go too quiet defensively. The back line and pivot lead the noise.
Communication — Team-Wide

When we don't have the ball, the team goes too quiet. That's a fix-it problem, not a personality problem.

  • Front three (STs + CAM) — coordinate the press. Are we going? Are we holding?
  • WBs and ball-side ST — call the wide press together.
  • Pivot and CBs — run the spine. Long balls, runners, the line.
  • GK — best view on the pitch. Use it. Be loud.
Forward Line

Striker

Two of you. Front of the press, end of every attack.

What You Do

You're the trigger man. Nothing in our defensive system works if the front two don't press together. In attack, you stretch their back line so the midfield has space to play.

The two of you have to be linked. One drops, one runs. One presses ball, one cuts the switch. Talk constantly.

Attack / Defend / Transition

In Attack — 3-5-2
  • Stay HIGH. Pin their back line — don't drop into midfield to get the ball.
  • One drops, one runs in behind. Decide as the ball is being played to our pivot. Don't both do the same thing.
  • Swap sides regularly. Don't camp on the same shoulder. If you've been on the left for 5 minutes, drift right. Make their CBs talk and switch.
  • Be unpredictable with your runs. Don't make the same run through the same channel every time. Mix it up — in behind, dropping in, across the back line, into the half-space. Keep their CBs guessing.
  • Get into the box. When the WB is crossing, you should already be in the 6-yard area or at the back post.
  • Run the channels. Between their CB and FB is your space.
In Defence — 5-3-2
  • You start every press. See the trigger → you go.
  • Ball-side ST presses the CB with the ball. Curve your run to block the pass to the other CB.
  • Far-side ST cuts the switch. Don't ball-watch.
  • Block the pass into their pivot/CDM. Use your body shape — your shadow decides where they can pass.
Transition
  • Won the ball: go in behind immediately. Ball is coming over the top — you should already be running.
  • Lost the ball: counter-press. DON'T jog back. Closest one to the ball goes — fast.

Engine Room

Double Pivot — DM & B2B

Two of you. The DM (Defensive Pivot) sits and screens. The B2B (Box-to-Box) covers ground.

What You Do

The two of you are the most important players on the pitch for our shape. If you're in position, the team is in shape. If both of you wander, we crack open.

You're not interchangeable. The DM sits deeper, the B2B is the more advanced of the two. Roles are different even though you start side by side.

DM (Defensive Pivot) — the deeper one. Sits in front of the back three. Screens passes into their forwards. Sweeps long balls. Recycles possession. Almost never leaves the central zone in front of our back line. The anchor.

B2B (Box-to-Box) — the more advanced one. Steps higher when we attack, supports the CAM. Covers more ground vertically. Steps up to press. First runner forward when we have control. The engine.

In attack the B2B pushes higher; the DM holds. In defence both screen the back line, but the DM is the first to drop on long balls.

System

Formations

5-3-2 defending. 3-5-2 attacking. Same players, different shape.

The wing-backs are the difference. When we have the ball, they push high and we look like a 3-5-2 with width. When we lose it, they drop into the back line and we're a 5-3-2.

Same eleven players. The wing-backs do the work to switch between the shapes.

5-3-2 Defending

  • Two STs stay at halfway — ready for transition
  • CAM sits ahead of the double pivot — three midfielders compact through the middle
  • Wing-backs drop into the back five
  • Wide CBs (the fastest two) cover the wingers
  • Central CB stays home — calls the line
  • GK off the line — sweeper-keeper position

3-5-2 Attacking

  • Three CBs split wide — wide CBs give the pivot passing options
  • Double pivot (DM deeper, B2B more advanced) sits ahead of the back three — the engine room
  • Wing-backs push HIGH and WIDE — they ARE our width
  • CAM in the pocket, between their lines
  • Two STs stretch their back line

Why Pace On The Outside CBs

This is the most important personnel decision in our shape. Get it right and the high line works. Get it wrong and we get pulled apart on transition.

  • Fastest two CBs go on the outside. They cover the channels and the wingers when WBs are caught forward. They have to be willing to sprint 30m+ on a long ball over the top.
  • Smart, organised CB stays central. They run the line, call the trap, and pick up runners through the middle. Brain over pace.
  • WBs need stamina above all else. Up and down all game. If they can't last 90, the back five becomes a back four every time we lose them.

System

Pressing Triggers

If everyone presses on different cues, no one's pressing together.

Pressing only works if it's coordinated. One player going alone gives them a free man.

These triggers are the only signals that mean GO. If you see one, the press is on. If you don't, hold shape.

When To Press (GO)

✓
Heavy Touch

Their player gets the ball away from their body. Looking down to find it. Your moment.

✓
Pass Back to CB

Ball goes backwards. Receiver facing own goal. Press while ball is travelling.

✓
Facing Own Goal

Their player gets ball with back to play. Pin them, don't let them turn.

✓
Square Pass CB to CB

They go sideways. Closest ST jumps the receiver. Other ST blocks the way back.

✓
Ball into Wide Channel

In our mid-block, ball goes into wide channel = trap. Ball-side WB jumps to press.

When NOT To Press (HOLD)

✗
CB On Half-Turn, Eyes Up

Set, scanning, with options ahead. They'll just pass around you. Hold shape.

✗
You're The Only One Going

If you press alone, free man for them. Look at teammates first.

First Wave

  • Two STs — front of the press
  • CAM — third presser, blocks the pivot
  • Ball-side WB — jumps to press their FB
  • Far-side WB — tucks in, becomes 4th defender

Second Wave (If First Is Beaten)

  • Closer pivot steps up to press whoever has the ball in midfield. The other pivot covers.
  • Wide CB on ball side slides to support
  • Central CB and other wide CB hold the line
  • WB who stepped up sprints back into the five
  • Whole back line drops together — never split

System

Mid-Block

When we're not full pressing and not camped. Most of the game is here.

We can't press for 90 minutes. We can't sit in our box for 90 minutes. Most of the game we're somewhere in between — that's the mid-block.

Compact 5-3-2 in our half but not deep. We force them wide, then trigger the press once they're in the trap.

Shape

How It Works

  • Compact through the middle. 5-3-2 stays narrow. Middle third closed.
  • Force them wide. Their ball-side full-back is the only easy pass — by design.
  • Wide channels are the trap. The ball-side WB is the trap-springer — the moment the ball goes into their channel, the WB jumps and presses.
  • STs screen the pivot. Block easy pass forward through the middle.
  • Don't get drawn out. Let them have the ball in front, not through.
  • Once trigger appears — back-pass, heavy touch, ball into wide channel — go full press.

Common Mistakes

Pressing aloneFront two go, rest stay = they pass around you, 9 vs 11.
Sitting too deepTheir pivot has all day. Stay just inside our half.
No triggerBall into the wide channel and no one presses = they switch. Pounce when it goes wide.
Ball-watchingFar-side WB and pivot need to stay narrow. Everyone shifting ball-side = the switch kills us.

System

Transitions

The 5 seconds after we win or lose the ball. Games are decided here.

The first 5 seconds after a turnover decide everything. Their team is unbalanced. So is ours. Whoever reacts faster wins.

We attack fast. We recover fast. No jogging.

Won The Ball — Going Forward

  • First look forward. Don't recycle backwards if there's a forward option.
  • STs already high — they should be running in behind as we win it.
  • WBs sprint forward — provide the width.
  • CAM drives at their back line — break their lines.
  • Hit them before they're set. Five seconds.
  • If no fast forward option — keep it, build again, don't give it back.

Lost The Ball — Recovery

  • Closest player counter-presses immediately. Don't dive in — delay them, buy time.
  • WBs SPRINT back into the five. If you stepped up, you have most ground to recover.
  • Wide CBs use pace to cover the counter. Goal-side fast.
  • Back line drops together — never split. Central CB calls it.
  • Midfield three reform in front of the back line.
  • STs come back if not the closest counter-presser.

Common Mistakes

Jogging backJog when we lose it = the goal is on us. Sprint or get changed.
Going backwardsWe win it, you pass backwards = wasted moment. Look forward first.
Diving inCounter-pressing is about delaying, not winning. Stay on your feet.
Splitting the back lineOne CB drops, one holds = striker onside. Drop together. ALWAYS.

System

Build-Up & Goal Kicks

How we start attacks from the back. With and without their press.

We're not a long-ball team. When we have time, we play out. When we don't, we go long — but with intent, not just hoofing it.

Two scenarios cover most of the game: they press us or they don't.

Default Build-Up Shape (No Pressure)

  • Wide CBs split VERY wide — go to the touchline area. They're our build-up width.
  • Central CB drops slightly — sits between the wide CBs.
  • Double pivot drops in front of the back three. Forms a 5-man build: 3 CBs + 2 pivots.
  • WBs push high — give the GK and CBs an option up the line.
  • CAM finds the pocket between their lines.
  • STs stay HIGH — pin their back line, give us depth.

The Pass Order (When Time)

  • 1. GK to wide CB — easy first pass.
  • 2. Wide CB to WB or pivot — go forward through the half-space.
  • 3. One pivot drops if pressed — the OTHER stays. Switch to other side.
  • 4. Long ball to ST — only if no other option.

If They Press Us

If their forwards are aggressive on our build-up, we have three options:

Option 1 — Beat the press, play out
  • One pivot drops between the wide CBs — gives a third deep player. The OTHER pivot stays higher.
  • Look for the free man. If they press 3 with 2, someone is free.
  • One-touch passing — don't let them set the trap.
Option 2 — Long ball with intent
  • GK or wide CB hits a long diagonal to the far ST.
  • Other ST attacks the second ball.
  • Pivot and CAM push up to win the second ball area.
  • WBs push high ready for knockdowns.
Option 3 — Play out wide and go forward
  • GK to wide CB.
  • Wide CB to WB who has dropped to receive.
  • WB plays it forward early — long ball down the line for the ST to chase.

Goal Kick Routine

Specific shape from goal kicks:

  • Both wide CBs in the box corners — give GK two short options.
  • One pivot on the edge of the box — pivot for the next pass.
  • Central CB on the D — third option, helps recycle.
  • WBs already high and wide.
  • STs stretching their back line.
  • If they don't press → play out short.
  • If they press the box → GK goes long to the STs, second ball is everything.

Common Mistakes

Wide CBs too narrowGo to the touchline. The wider you are, the harder it is to press all of us.
Pivot not droppingIf they press 2v2 on the CBs, one of you has to drop in to make it 3v2.
STs dropping for ballStay high. If you drop, their CBs follow and we lose depth.
GK going long every timeIf they're not pressing, play out. Use the time.
No second-ball planLong ball without pivot and CAM pushing up to win the knockdown = they recover it.

How we adjust to what they're doing. Most teams in Div 2 play one of these shapes. For each: text matchups, plus animations showing how we defend AND attack against them.

Our base plan stays the same. But the way they set up changes WHERE we press and WHO has the most work.

Quick scan when the game starts: How many strikers? How many midfielders? Do they press or sit?

The Situation

Two strikers, four in midfield, four at the back. Most common shape in Div 2. Equal in midfield, we have spare CB at the back.

STs match their STs. CAM has free role. DM marks left CM, B2B marks right CM. Wide CBs cover wingers.

Our 3 CBs vs their 2 STs = always one free at the back. Use it. WBs HIGH and WIDE — wear out their wide mids.

Watch Out For

Long balls to their 2 strikers — wide CBs need to be alert. Their wingers cutting inside late.

Key Player

Wide CBs. Isolated 1v1 with their wingers all game. Pace and discipline.

System

Game-State Management

Adjusting based on the score. Same shape, different intent.

Don't play the whole 90 the same way. The score changes what we want from each phase.

1-0 Up — Last 20 Minutes

  • Don't invite pressure. Press hard when the trigger comes — but don't go chasing if it isn't there. Hold shape.
  • WBs stay deeper when we don't have the ball. Become wing-backs in a back five, not wingers.
  • Pivot drops a yard. Closer to the back line, screen everything central.
  • Keep the ball when we win it. Don't force the counter if the forward pass isn't there. Recycle and run the clock.
  • STs work the corners. If we win it, take it into the corner flag and force them to win it back.
  • Talk gets louder, not quieter. Tired teams go silent. Don't be that team.

1-0 Down — Last 20 Minutes

  • WBs go higher and stay higher. We need width and crosses. Let the wide CBs take the wider position.
  • Press higher up the pitch. Make their CBs uncomfortable. Force a mistake.
  • CAM pushes higher. Become a third striker. Pivot covers the space behind.
  • STs stay central. Don't drift wide — be in the box for crosses.
  • Win the second balls. Tall players go up for crosses and long throws. Pivot and CAM stay alert for knockdowns.
  • Risk is OK. One pivot can push forward. We can't lose 2-0 worse than 1-0.

Drawing With 10 Minutes Left

  • Decide before the whistle: are we pushing for the win or accepting the point? Coach calls it.
  • Pushing — press higher, WBs forward, CAM higher.
  • Accepting — hold shape, don't lose it. A draw is better than a counter that costs us the point.

Up By Two Or More

  • Don't relax. Keep doing what got us the lead.
  • Make subs to keep legs fresh. Don't go from 2-0 to 2-2 because we got tired.
  • Take the foot off when killing the clock makes sense — last 5 minutes, lead intact. Recycle, take it to corners.

Down By Two Or More

  • One goal at a time. Don't chase wildly. Get the next one and the game is back on.
  • Take more risk in possession — drive at them, find the runner, don't recycle.
  • Don't get cooked on the counter. Pivot stays disciplined. We can't go 3-0 down.
